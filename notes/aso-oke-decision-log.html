Why I Threw Away My 8-Epoch Whisper LoRA Fine-Tune

Pre-registered evaluation gates, discovering that the standard Yorùbá benchmark is 100% male, and why shipping a zero-shot Apache-2.0 model beat sunk-cost pride.

1. Write the pass mark before training starts

Most solo ML projects fail silently by moving the goalposts after seeing their first validation curve. When I started Aso Oke — a Yorùbá↔English speech-to-text and translation application built on a strict $400 budget ceiling — I wrote down every pass gate and kill criterion before spending a dollar on GPU compute.

First came the baseline. Running OpenAI's Whisper large-v3 zero-shot on the FLEURS Yorùbá test split (817 clips) produced a 92.6% word error rate (WER) — functionally unusable. Before touching any training loop, I also built a custom Unicode NFD diacritic normalizer validated against 200 real Yorùbá sentences: in a tonal language, ọ and o or ẹ and e change a word's meaning entirely, so sloppy text normalization corrupts both training targets and WER scoring.

2. The fine-tune improved 31 points — and still got rejected

Following standard fine-tuning playbooks, I trained an 8-epoch LoRA adapter on Whisper large-v3. WER dropped from 92.6% to 61.4% — a 31.2-point improvement that would look great in a LinkedIn chart.

The problem: 61.4% WER still failed my pre-registered ship gate. Rather than rationalizing the result, I evaluated Meta's Apache-2.0 omniASR_LLM_1B zero-shot on the exact same 817-clip test split. It landed at 32.0% WER — beating my fine-tune by nearly 30 points with zero training cost. The decision was immediate: shelve my own fine-tune and ship omniASR_LLM_1B.

3. Catching the 100%-male benchmark blind spot

While auditing FLEURS Yorùbá validation and test splits, I discovered that both splits are 100% male. Reporting only FLEURS WER would hide whether the model collapsed on women's voices. I assembled a 20-speaker community evaluation set and measured subgroup performance directly: 27.7% WER on unseen holdout speakers and 32.8% WER on women's voices — confirming the gains generalize beyond the public benchmark.

4. Licence audits & honest translation tradeoffs

In Week 1, a commercial licence audit caught a translation model restriction that would have permanently blocked charging for the product if discovered at launch. Swapping early to google/madlad400-3b-mt with a custom MAFAND LoRA adapter (published at donne4real/aso-oke-madlad-lora) kept the entire stack commercially clean.

On inference speed, early runs hit 7.4s p95 against a 3-second latency target. Merging the LoRA adapter weights in fp16 on Hugging Face ZeroGPU brought p95 latency down to 1.99s (yo→en) and 2.51s (en→yo) — a 3.7× speedup — paired with a 52-entry proper-noun post-processing glossary.

Radical honesty on the translation quality gate

Translation BLEU currently measures 11.7 / 8.0 against a pre-registered 25-point gate. Rather than hiding that gap or cherry-picking examples, Aso Oke launches on 13 Dec 2026 with a visible quality disclaimer on translation while speech recognition ships at full confidence.
